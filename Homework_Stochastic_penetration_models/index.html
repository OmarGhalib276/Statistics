Stochastic Penetration Models

System Compromise Simulation & Asymptotic Analysis

> Theoretical Framework

Consider a network infrastructure comprising \( n \) systems under attack by \( m \) independent attackers. We model the penetration process as a stochastic process with independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) increments \( X_1, X_2, \dots \), where each \( X_i \) represents the penetration score achieved at the \( i \)-th step.

1. Target Models

  • Bernoulli Model: \( X_i \in \{0, 1\} \) with \( P(X_i = 1) = p \). This asymmetric model dictates that an attacker either successfully compromises a system (1) with probability \( p \), or fails entirely (0).
  • Rademacher Model: \( X_i \in \{-1, +1\} \) with \( P(X_i = +1) = P(X_i = -1) = \frac{1}{2} \). This symmetric, zero-mean model represents a balanced scenario, introduced to study pure random walks in security environments (e.g., an attacker gains or loses ground with equal probability).

2. Asymptotic Behavior & Normalizations

For each simulation trajectory, we study the behavior of three primary quantities as \( n \) increases:

  • Total Score (\( S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i \)):
    This is the raw aggregate score. Because the increments are independent, the variance grows linearly with \( n \) (\( \text{Var}(S_n) = n \sigma^2 \)). Consequently, macroscopic trajectories naturally fan out, demonstrating unbounded variance as time approaches infinity.
  • Average Score (\( \frac{S_n}{n} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i \)):
    According to the Law of Large Numbers (LLN), the average score converges in probability to the expected value \( \mu \) (\( p \) for Bernoulli, 0 for Rademacher). The variance shrinks to zero (\( \frac{\sigma^2}{n} \)), indicating that long-term average behavior becomes highly deterministic.
    \[ \lim_{n \to \infty} P\left( \left| \frac{S_n}{n} - \mu \right| > \epsilon \right) = 0 \]
  • Normalized Total Score (\( \frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i \)):
    Normalizing by \( \sqrt{n} \) perfectly stabilizes the variance. According to the Central Limit Theorem (CLT), in the Rademacher case (where \( \mu=0, \sigma^2=1 \)), the distribution of this normalized score converges in distribution to a Standard Normal probability curve:
    \[ \frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,1) \]

> Cybersecurity Interpretation

From an operational standpoint, simulating \( n \) systems against \( m \) attackers yields crucial insights for threat forecasting and risk assessment:

  • System Size (\( n \)): As the size of the infrastructure grows, the Law of Large Numbers ensures that the average compromise rate approaches the mathematical probability of a single vulnerability. Localized anomalies are absorbed by the massive scale, allowing defenders to estimate the total compromised baseline with high accuracy.
  • Number of Attackers (\( m \)): A larger cohort of attackers provides empirical proof of the Central Limit Theorem. While a single attacker's trajectory is completely unpredictable (Total Score divergence), analyzing \( m \) independent attack vectors reveals that the distribution of overall outcomes forms a predictable, mathematical bell curve.
  • Anomaly Detection: The Normalized Score exposes the magnitude of deviation from the expected attack progression. Because it converges to a Normal distribution, Security Operation Centers (SOCs) can employ standard deviation thresholds (e.g., \( \pm 3\sigma \)) to distinguish between normal stochastic noise and highly coordinated Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs).
